# Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension 1–5. Anchor every score to the descriptors below and quote evidence from the sample. Half points are allowed when a sample sits clearly between anchors.

## Clarity & concision

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Every sentence's meaning is unmistakable on first read. No filler; each word earns its place. Complex ideas are made simple without being dumbed down. |
| 4 | Meaning is almost always clear. Occasional wordiness or a vague pronoun, but nothing that forces rereading. |
| 3 | Generally understandable, but the reader must reread several passages. Noticeable padding, hedging, or abstraction where concreteness was possible. |
| 2 | Frequent ambiguity. Buried subjects, stacked nominalizations, unexplained jargon. The main point of some paragraphs is unclear. |
| 1 | The reader cannot reliably determine what the writer means. |

Look for: buried verbs (nominalizations), expletive openers ("There is/are"), vague referents ("this" without a noun), redundant pairs ("each and every"), jargon mismatched to audience, hedging stacks ("it could perhaps be argued").

## Structure & organization

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | A clear through-line from opening to close. Each paragraph advances one idea and hands off cleanly to the next. Openings hook; endings land. |
| 4 | Sound overall architecture with minor lapses — one misplaced paragraph, a transition that jumps. |
| 3 | The main idea is findable but the path wanders. Some paragraphs mix ideas; ordering feels arbitrary in places. |
| 2 | No discernible plan. The reader must impose organization themselves. Key claims appear without setup or disappear without payoff. |
| 1 | Disconnected fragments of thought; no unity at any level. |

Look for: identifiable thesis or controlling purpose, topic sentences, paragraph unity, transition quality, proportionality (space allocated matching importance), setup/payoff integrity, strength of opening and closing.

## Grammar & mechanics

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Error-free or so close that remaining slips are typos, not knowledge gaps. Punctuation is used for meaning, not decoration. |
| 4 | A few minor errors that never impede meaning (a comma splice, an inconsistent hyphenation). |
| 3 | Recurring error patterns (subject–verb agreement, tense drift, apostrophe misuse) that a careful reader notices but can read past. |
| 2 | Errors frequent enough to slow or occasionally derail comprehension. |
| 1 | Errors dominate; decoding requires effort. |

Count *patterns*, not instances — three comma splices are one finding. Distinguish errors from register-appropriate informality (sentence fragments in marketing copy are not errors).

## Style & voice

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | A distinctive, consistent voice suited to the audience. Varied sentence rhythm used deliberately. Word choice is fresh and precise; figures of speech illuminate rather than decorate. |
| 4 | Pleasant, competent prose with occasional flashes of distinctiveness. Register consistent. |
| 3 | Serviceable but anonymous. Monotonous sentence shapes, stock phrasing, safe word choices. Occasional register wobble. |
| 2 | Style works against the content — strained metaphors, thesaurus-driven diction, clashing registers, or relentless monotony. |
| 1 | No control of tone or diction; style actively obscures meaning. |

Look for: sentence-length variance, sentence-opening variety, register consistency, cliché density, concrete vs. abstract diction balance, whether rhetorical devices (parallelism, triads, metaphor) serve the argument.

## Readability & audience fit

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Quantitative readability sits squarely in the target band for the stated audience, and qualitative signals (assumed knowledge, terminology, examples) match it too. |
| 4 | Slightly above or below the target band, or terminology occasionally overshoots the audience, with no real harm. |
| 3 | A full grade band off target, or a mismatch between assumed knowledge and the audience's actual knowledge in several places. |
| 2 | The audience would struggle or feel condescended to for large stretches. |
| 1 | Written for a different audience entirely. |

This dimension requires the metrics in `readability-metrics.md` plus judgment: numbers alone never set the score.

## Overall score

Weighted average, then round to one decimal:

- Clarity & concision — 30%
- Structure & organization — 25%
- Style & voice — 20%
- Grammar & mechanics — 15%
- Readability & audience fit — 10%

Adjust weights when the genre demands it (e.g., grammar rises to 25% for legal or academic submission drafts; style rises to 30% for fiction) and state any adjustment in the report.

## Verdict bands

| Overall | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 4.5–5.0 | Publication-ready; only polish remains |
| 3.5–4.4 | Strong; one focused revision pass from excellent |
| 2.5–3.4 | Competent draft; needs substantive revision |
| 1.5–2.4 | Early draft; needs structural rework before line editing is worthwhile |
| 1.0–1.4 | Restart from an outline |
