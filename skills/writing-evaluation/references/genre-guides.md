# Genre Expectations

Genre changes what counts as good. Before scoring, identify the genre, then apply the adjustments below. If the sample doesn't fit any listed genre, reason by analogy and state your assumptions.

## Persuasive / opinion essay

- Reward: a defensible, specific thesis; steel-manned counterarguments; evidence woven into (not appended to) claims; momentum toward the conclusion.
- Penalize: thesis restated instead of advanced; strawmen; appeals piled up without warrant.
- Convention allowances: first person, rhetorical questions (sparingly), emphatic short sentences.

## Academic writing

- Reward: precise claims with scoped hedging ("in this sample," not "always"); signposted structure; terminology used consistently; citations integrated grammatically.
- Penalize: unscoped generalizations; elegant variation applied to technical terms (call the same thing the same name); throat-clearing introductions.
- Convention allowances: passive voice in methods sections; higher FKGL; nominalizations where they name established concepts.
- Weight shift: Grammar & mechanics 15% → 25% for submission drafts.

## Business & professional (emails, memos, reports)

- Reward: the ask or bottom line in the first three sentences (BLUF); scannable structure; concrete next steps with owners and dates; register matched to the relationship.
- Penalize: burying the request; hedged asks ("it might be good if someone could maybe…"); corporate filler ("touch base," "circle back," "leverage synergies").
- Convention allowances: fragments in bullet lists; headings in even short documents.

## Technical writing / documentation

- Reward: task orientation (what the reader does, in order); one term per concept; examples for every abstraction; accurate imperative steps.
- Penalize: describing the system when the reader needs a procedure; ambiguous step ordering; untested-looking instructions.
- Convention allowances: second person imperative; sentence fragments in tables; FKGL is nearly irrelevant — evaluate task completability instead.

## Fiction & creative nonfiction

- Reward: a voice you could pick out of a lineup; concrete sensory detail; dialogue that characterizes; scenes that carry meaning without explaining it; controlled rhythm.
- Penalize: told emotions ("she felt sad") where showing was available; filtering ("he saw," "she noticed"); adverb-propped dialogue tags; purple patches that stop the story.
- Convention allowances: fragments, comma splices, and nonstandard grammar as deliberate technique — judge by whether the effect lands, not by the rule broken.
- Weight shift: Style & voice 20% → 30%; Readability & audience fit → 5%.

## Cover letters & personal statements

- Reward: specific evidence for every claimed trait; knowledge of the particular institution/company; a voice that sounds like one real person; ruthless length discipline.
- Penalize: adjective résumés ("I am a passionate, driven self-starter"); generic letters with the name swapped; narrating the attached résumé.

## Marketing & copywriting

- Reward: a single clear promise; benefit-first framing; rhythm built for skimming; a concrete call to action.
- Penalize: feature lists without benefits; superlative stacking; cleverness that obscures the offer.
- Convention allowances: fragments, one-sentence paragraphs, starting with conjunctions, FRE well above typical prose.

## Social / informal writing

- Evaluate against effectiveness for its platform and audience, not formal standards. Lowercase style, emoji, and slang are register choices; judge consistency and effect.
